### 3.2 Interest mechanics

Each rated class accrues interest at three-month Term SOFR plus its contractual spread, subject to a 0.00% floor, using Actual/360. Interest accrues from the relevant Payment Date to the next Payment Date. The benchmark is reset for each interest period. Principal is not scheduled to amortize during the Reinvestment Period except where required by the transaction waterfall, coverage-test diversion, redemption or another mandatory event.

Interest on Classes A-1 through E is subject to the priority of the Interest Waterfall. A junior class may experience an interest shortfall or permitted deferral when the transaction's coverage provisions require cash to be redirected to more senior classes. Such a diversion is a structural protection mechanism and is not, by itself, an Event of Default.

## 6. Eligibility Requirements

### 6.1 Core eligibility standard

An Eligible Collateral Obligation must be a permitted corporate debt obligation whose acquisition is consistent with the portfolio's credit, maturity, currency, pricing and documentation requirements. Permitted assets include senior secured first-lien term loans, certain second-lien loans, qualifying unsecured loans, revolving commitments, delayed-draw term loans and participation interests. Cash equivalents and other permitted investments may be held for liquidity.

At acquisition, an ordinary loan must have no more than 7.50 years remaining to stated maturity and must generally be rated at least B3/B- or satisfy the manager's permitted credit-estimate procedures. The minimum contractual spread for ordinary floating-rate assets is 2.90%. Purchase price is generally required to be at least 72.5% of par, with specified exceptions for distressed or discounted obligations already subject to the transaction's adjustment rules.

### 6.2 Restricted asset categories

Defaulted Obligations are not ordinarily eligible as new purchases. Existing positions may be retained, exchanged or worked out when permitted. Fixed-rate assets are eligible within a 9.0% concentration cap. Second-lien assets are subject to an 8.0% limit, while unsecured obligations are subject to a 5.5% limit. Participation interests must provide sufficient transferability and enforceable economic rights.

Revolving commitments and delayed-draw facilities may be purchased where the manager reasonably expects that the Transaction can fund required draws without impairing senior note protection. Long-dated assets, low-priced obligations and assets with unusual settlement mechanics receive separate scrutiny under the eligibility and concentration provisions.

### 7.2 Breach and cure

Concentration tests are generally measured on each Payment Date and after specified portfolio events. A breach caused by a manager-directed purchase is treated differently from a breach caused by repayment, rating migration, market movement or another involuntary event. The manager is required to use commercially reasonable efforts to cure a breach and may be restricted from acquiring additional exposure to the affected category.

The cure process may involve sales, substitutions, paydowns or the passage of time. A cure does not require the manager to maximize trading volume; it requires restoration of the contractual metric while preserving the interests of the Transaction as a whole. A concentration limit is not a prediction of the actual final portfolio composition.

### 9.2 Calculation and consequences

The Calculation Agent measures the OC tests using the Adjusted Collateral Principal Balance and the applicable outstanding note balances. If an OC test fails, principal otherwise available to junior classes or subordinated interests is redirected to the affected senior classes according to the applicable priority. The diversion continues until the test is cured or the relevant principal proceeds have been exhausted.

IC tests compare specified Interest Proceeds with required interest obligations. If a Class A or Class B IC test fails, cash is redirected to satisfy senior interest before amounts may flow to junior classes. A failure of a junior IC test can similarly affect distributions to the subordinated interests without necessarily disturbing senior interest already paid.

### 9.3 Test timing

OC and IC tests are calculated on each Payment Date and at other specified times, including certain redemption, refinancing and enforcement events. The Effective Date values are baseline figures, not forecasts. A test is cured only when the defined calculation again satisfies the required threshold.

## 10. Interest Diversion Test

### 10.1 Oakmont diversion framework

During the Reinvestment Period, Oakmont applies an Interest Diversion Test with a required level of 103.5%. The test uses a specified combination of collateral quality, coverage and portfolio metrics. It is intentionally separate from the ordinary IC tests and provides an additional mechanism for retaining cash within the collateral structure.

### 10.2 Effect of a failure

If the Interest Diversion Test is below 103.5% on a measurement date, the specified portion of otherwise distributable interest is redirected from subordinated interests into Principal Proceeds. During the Reinvestment Period, diverted amounts may be reinvested only if the applicable reinvestment criteria are satisfied; otherwise they follow the principal application priority. The diversion stops when the test is cured under the contractual formula.

## 11. Interest Waterfall

### 11.1 Priority of Interest Proceeds

Interest Proceeds are first applied to taxes, registered-office costs and other senior administrative obligations. Next come Trustee, Calculation Agent, Paying Agent, Collateral Administrator and Custodian expenses. The waterfall then pays the management fee and permitted hedge or counterparty amounts. Thereafter, available interest is applied to Class A-1 and Class A-2, followed by Classes B, C, D and E, subject to the coverage-test and interest-diversion provisions.

Residual interest may be distributed to Subordinated Interests only after the required senior and junior rated-note amounts and diversions have been addressed. The ordering is contractual and may produce different economic outcomes depending on whether the Transaction is in normal compliance, test deficiency or post-default enforcement.

### 11.2 Shortfalls and deferrals

If available Interest Proceeds are insufficient, payment follows the priority established by the waterfall. A junior class may receive less than its accrued amount even where senior classes are paid in full. Where interest deferral is expressly permitted, the deferred amount remains subject to the note-specific provisions governing subsequent payment.

## 12. Principal Waterfall and Reinvestment

### 12.1 Principal collections

Principal Proceeds include scheduled amortization, sales, recoveries and other amounts designated as principal. Through the end of the Reinvestment Period on May 20, 2029, the manager may generally reinvest Principal Proceeds in qualifying assets. Purchases must satisfy the eligibility criteria, concentration limits and required collateral-quality conditions.

### 12.2 Trading policy

The manager may sell an asset to capture value, reduce credit risk, manage concentration, address rating migration, fund a workout or improve portfolio construction. Sales are not required to maximize short-term market value if a different action better serves the Transaction's longer-term interests. Substitutions are permitted where an asset becomes ineligible or otherwise satisfies a contractual substitution condition.

### 12.3 Post-reinvestment waterfall

After May 20, 2029, ordinary reinvestment ends. Principal is then applied sequentially to A-1 and A-2, then B, C, D and E, with residual amounts ultimately available to Subordinated Interests. Limited trading may continue for workouts, asset substitutions, concentration cures, credit deterioration and other specified purposes.

## 13. Reinvestment Criteria and Trading Restrictions

### 13.1 Reinvestment tests

A replacement asset must satisfy the applicable eligibility rules at acquisition and may not knowingly cause a prohibited concentration breach. The manager is also expected to maintain the portfolio's quality profile, including WARF, WAS, WAL, diversity and low-rated exposure. A trade that improves spread but causes an impermissible maturity or rating deterioration is not a compliant reinvestment.

### 13.2 Sale proceeds and substitutions

Sale proceeds can be reinvested during the Reinvestment Period when all conditions are satisfied. A substitution may replace an asset following a credit event, documentation problem or other permitted circumstance. The replacement does not receive automatic eligibility merely because the original asset was eligible; each new asset must be tested independently.

### 13.3 Termination of active reinvestment

The Reinvestment Period terminates on May 20, 2029. After that date, ordinary portfolio turnover is curtailed. The manager remains responsible for managing defaults, restructurings and permitted dispositions, but cannot treat the post-reinvestment period as an unrestricted continuation of the original trading mandate.

## 14. Redemption, Refinancing and Reset

## 17. Events of Default and Remedies

### 17.1 Events of default

Events of Default include specified failures to pay senior note principal or interest after applicable grace periods, insolvency or bankruptcy of the Issuer or Co-Issuer, and material breaches of representations or covenants that remain uncured. Following an Event of Default, the Trustee may take the actions authorized by the transaction documents, including acceleration, enforcement of collateral security and liquidation of assets. ### 17.3 Post-acceleration priority

## 18. Voting and Amendment Provisions

### 18.1 Ordinary amendments

Extraordinary matters generally require at least 75% consent of the affected voting interests, with higher or class-specific requirements for protected economic terms. ### 18.3 Manager replacement and equity rights

Subordinated Interests have specified voting and direction rights, including rights associated with optional redemption and certain manager matters. ## 20. Risk Factors

### 20.1 Credit, default and recovery risk

The portfolio consists primarily of below-investment-grade corporate obligations. Borrower defaults, restructurings, downgrades and covenant breaches may reduce Interest Proceeds and Principal Proceeds. Recoveries may be below the 53.0% initial assumption, especially where enterprise values decline or collateral is subordinated to other claims.

### 20.2 Interest-rate and reinvestment risk

Most collateral and all rated notes use floating-rate mechanics, but asset spreads, floors and reset dates can differ. Changes in Term SOFR can alter net interest margins. During the 30-month Reinvestment Period, replacement assets may carry lower spreads, longer maturities or weaker ratings than assets originally purchased.

### 20.3 Liquidity and concentration risk

Loans may be illiquid and may not be saleable at the price assumed by the manager. Concentration limits reduce but do not eliminate correlated losses. Industry, geographic and sponsor-level stress can affect multiple obligors simultaneously.

### 20.4 Counterparty and operational risk

Failure of a trustee, custodian, account bank, hedge counterparty or service provider could interrupt payments or reporting. Operational errors can create timing mismatches or require correction. The transaction depends on accurate data from multiple parties.

### 20.5 Structural and subordination risk

The capital structure allocates losses in order from Subordinated Interests through the rated classes. Coverage-test failures can redirect cash away from junior investors. Senior noteholders may be paid while junior classes receive reduced or no distributions.

### 20.6 Redemption, manager and regulatory risk

Optional redemption can shorten the expected life of the notes, while market conditions can make refinancing uneconomic. The manager's investment and workout decisions materially affect performance. Changes in tax, securities, banking or regulatory requirements may alter transaction costs or permitted activities.

### 20.7 Foreign-currency risk

Permitted non-U.S.-dollar assets can create foreign-exchange exposure. Hedging arrangements reduce but may not eliminate basis, timing and counterparty risk. Currency movements can affect the realized value of otherwise eligible collateral.
