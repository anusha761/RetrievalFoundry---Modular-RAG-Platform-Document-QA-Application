## 5. Collateral Eligibility

### 5.1 General eligibility

An Eligible Collateral Obligation must generally be a corporate loan or other permitted debt obligation acquired in a commercially reasonable transaction and satisfy maturity, currency, rating, spread, price and settlement requirements. A collateral obligation may be denominated in U.S. dollars, euros, pounds sterling or Canadian dollars. Non-U.S.-dollar assets are permitted only when currency exposure is addressed through the hedge and adjustment mechanics. An obligation generally may not have more than 8.0 years remaining to stated maturity at purchase. A minimum purchase price of 72.0% of par applies unless a permitted discounted-obligation exception is satisfied.

### 5.2 Credit and structural restrictions

A purchased loan should have a minimum issuer rating of B3/B- or better, subject to rules for unrated assets and credit estimates. Fixed-rate assets may be acquired only within the fixed-rate cap. Participation interests require satisfactory documentation of payment rights, transferability and settlement.

Revolving commitments are eligible where the manager reasonably expects the Transaction can meet funded and unfunded obligations without impairing liquidity. Delayed-draw obligations are included in the applicable concentration test. No acquisition may intentionally cause a mandatory concentration limit to be breached unless expressly permitted as a cure.

### 8.2 Consequences of failure

If an OC test fails, principal that would otherwise be distributed to junior classes or subordinated interests is redirected to repay the most senior class whose protection is implicated, continuing until the test is cured or the applicable payment priority is exhausted. A failed Class A test therefore has the most restrictive consequence.

IC tests operate through the interest waterfall. A failure can redirect available interest away from subordinated interests and toward the affected note classes. Cure amounts are determined using the transaction definitions rather than by comparing simple headline portfolio balances.

## 9. Interest Diversion and Distribution Controls

### 9.1 Interest Diversion Test

The Interest Diversion Test applies during the Reinvestment Period. Its required level is 103.5% of the specified benchmark. If the test is below the required level, a specified portion of excess interest otherwise distributable to subordinated interests is converted into Principal Proceeds. During the Reinvestment Period, those proceeds may support qualifying purchases; otherwise they are applied toward the most senior class permitted under the cure mechanics.

### 9.2 Interest waterfall

Interest Proceeds are applied first to taxes, registered-office charges and issuer-level expenses with priority. Next come trustee, calculation-agent, collateral-administration and paying-agent fees, followed by senior management fees and permitted hedge or counterparty payments. Interest is then paid sequentially to Class A-1, Class A-2, Class B, Class C, Class D and Class E. Required coverage-test cures and Interest Diversion amounts are handled under their respective provisions, followed by residual distributions to subordinated interests.

The Transaction is therefore not a pro rata interest structure. A junior class may receive no current-period distribution even while senior classes are paid in full, particularly when an OC or IC test is below its required level.

## 10. Principal Waterfall and Reinvestment

### 10.1 Principal collections

Principal Proceeds include scheduled amortization, permitted sale proceeds, recoveries and other amounts characterized as principal. During the Reinvestment Period, qualifying principal can generally be reinvested. Outside the Reinvestment Period, principal is applied to the rated notes in sequential order unless a special provision applies.

### 10.2 Reinvestment Period

The Reinvestment Period terminates on June 20, 2029. The manager may trade to improve credit quality, preserve spread, manage concentration, respond to ratings changes or replace unsuitable assets. Each resulting portfolio must satisfy the eligibility criteria and portfolio-level tests.

The manager may not use trades to circumvent a concentration cap or extend collateral maturity beyond permitted limits. After the Reinvestment Period, sales are principally permitted for credit-risk management, workouts, amendments, clean-up activities and other expressly permitted purposes.

### 10.3 Post-reinvestment amortization

Following June 20, 2029, principal collections are ordinarily applied first to Class A-1 and then A-2, followed by Classes B, C, D and E. Subordinated interests receive residual amounts only after rated notes and required expenses are satisfied. This sequential mechanism accelerates senior deleveraging and can alter the expected lives of junior classes.

## 17. Principal Risk Considerations

### 17.1 Credit, default and recovery risk

The collateral consists primarily of below-investment-grade obligations. An obligor may fail to pay, enter bankruptcy, restructure its debt or experience a decline in enterprise value. Recoveries may be substantially below the 54.0% portfolio assumption. A series of defaults can reduce the adjusted collateral balance and cause coverage tests to fail.

### 17.2 Market and reinvestment risk

Loan prices can decline rapidly during market stress, and liquidity can disappear when sales are most necessary. The 30-month Reinvestment Period exposes the Transaction to the risk that replacement loans offer lower spreads or weaker credit characteristics. The manager may also be required to sell attractive assets to cure a limit.

### 17.3 Structural and subordination risk

The notes are not equally protected. Senior notes receive payment before junior notes, and subordinated interests absorb losses first. Coverage-test failures can materially extend or shorten expected lives of different classes.

### 17.4 Counterparty, manager and regulatory risk

Failure by a trustee, custodian, hedge counterparty or other service provider could disrupt administration or cash flows. Manager replacement or loss of key personnel may affect trading decisions. Changes in tax, securities, banking, capital or CLO regulations may alter economics. Foreign-currency assets create exchange-rate and hedging considerations.

### 17.5 Redemption and refinancing risk

Optional redemption or refinancing may occur earlier than legal final maturity. Early repayment can reduce future spread income and change investment opportunities. A refinancing may also be unavailable during stressed market conditions, leaving the original notes outstanding longer than expected.
