## 8. Interest Diversion Test

An Interest Diversion Test applies beginning on the first Payment Date after the Effective Date. The required level is 105.0% of the specified diversion benchmark. If the test is below 105.0%, a portion of otherwise distributable interest proceeds attributable to the subordinated interests is diverted into principal proceeds and used to purchase additional Eligible Collateral Obligations or repay the most senior outstanding class, depending on whether the Transaction is within the Reinvestment Period. The diversion ceases when the test is restored above the cure level on a subsequent Measurement Date.

## 9. Interest and Principal Waterfalls

Interest proceeds are applied in the following order, subject to the detailed priority-of-payments provisions: (1) taxes, registered office and issuer expenses; (2) trustee, calculation, collateral-administration and paying-agent expenses; (3) senior management fees; (4) hedge and other senior counterparty amounts; (5) Class A-1 interest; (6) Class A-2 interest; (7) Class B interest; (8) Class C interest; (9) Class D interest; (10) Class E interest; (11) any required interest-diversion amounts; and (12) remaining subordinated/equity distributions. A coverage-test failure can change the treatment of steps 11 and 12 and may cause amounts otherwise payable to junior interests to be retained or redirected.

Principal proceeds received during the Reinvestment Period may be reinvested in qualifying assets if the purchase satisfies the reinvestment criteria, including collateral quality, concentration and weighted-average-life requirements. After the Reinvestment Period, principal collections are generally applied sequentially to Class A-1, Class A-2, Class B, Class C, Class D and Class E, with residual amounts thereafter available to subordinated interests. Workout proceeds and other special principal collections may be subject to separate provisions.

## 17. Risk Factors

Interest-rate and spread risk. The notes and collateral may reference different benchmarks or reset dates. Changes in benchmark levels, loan spreads or hedge economics can affect available cash flow. Reinvestment and liquidity risk. There is no assurance that the manager can source replacement assets at attractive spreads or sell deteriorating positions without losses. Concentration risk. Limits reduce but do not eliminate exposure to particular obligors, industries or regions.

Regulatory and foreign-currency risk. Changes in law, tax treatment, bank regulation or CLO-market requirements may affect transaction economics.
